Engineering screening tools

These browser-based tools support transparent first-pass calculations. They do not transmit or save your inputs. Every result is derived, screening-level evidence: use actual fluid properties, geometry, operating state, pressure drop, controls, safety requirements, and validation before design or procurement.

All inputs use SI units. Use the nomenclature for shared symbols and sign conventions, then record the system boundary, source, units, and evidence status of each input in the assumption and evidence register.

Allowable thermal resistance

$$R_{\mathrm{th,allow}} = \frac{T_{\mathrm{lim}} - T_{\mathrm{sink}}}{\dot{Q}_{\mathrm{load}}}$$

Resistance-network temperature rise

$$\Delta T = \dot{Q}_{\mathrm{load}}R_{\mathrm{th}}$$

Single-phase liquid-flow target

$$\dot{m} = \frac{\dot{Q}_{\mathrm{load}}}{c_p\Delta T_{\mathrm{fluid}}}$$

Sensible thermal-storage mass

$$m = \frac{E_{\mathrm{th}}}{c_p\Delta T}$$

Declared-boundary COP

$$\mathrm{COP}_{\mathrm{R}} = \frac{\dot{Q}_{\mathrm{cooling}}}{\dot{W}_{\mathrm{input}}}$$

Interpretation and limits

  • A thermal-resistance result represents the stated source-to-sink path; it does not establish a junction, interface, local-fluid, or component-case temperature unless those locations and resistances are explicitly in the model.
  • The liquid-flow calculation gives an average energy-balance target. It does not determine pump head, local convection, branch distribution, pressure drop, cavitation margin, material compatibility, or component temperature.
  • The storage calculation assumes all stored energy reaches a uniform thermal mass over the stated temperature change. It ignores rate limits, packaging, heat loss, and recharge.
  • The COP calculation is comparable only when cooling duty, electrical input, source/sink state, part-load condition, and time basis match.
