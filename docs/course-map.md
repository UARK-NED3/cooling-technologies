# Course map

## Learning sequence

The course begins with the required temperature control, rather than an equipment label. That ordering matters: a fan, a cold plate, a vapor-compression system, and thermal storage may all be plausible answers to different versions of the same problem, but their feasibility follows from the heat source, temperature level, environment, and allowable resources.

Before beginning a calculation, consult the [nomenclature and notation](nomenclature.md). It is the reference for the symbols and sign conventions used across the modules and engineering workspace.

```text
heat generation + temperature limit + environment
                    |
                    v
         heat-flow paths and thermal resistances
                    |
                    v
        mechanisms and system architectures
                    |
                    v
       performance metrics and resource tradeoffs
                    |
                    v
       screening decision + evidence needed for design
                    |
                    v
      optional transient model + technology evidence map
```

## Module deliverables

| Module | Learner deliverable | Evidence status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Heat-source and temperature-limit statement | User-defined / assumed |
| 2 | Thermal-resistance network and heat-rate estimate | Derived, screening-level |
| 3 | Mechanism comparison table | Inferred from stated conditions |
| 4 | Component-and-loop architecture sketch | Proposed |
| 5 | Performance metric table | Derived / reported inputs required |
| 6 | One-page cooling concept memo | Proposed; not a design approval |
| 7 | One-node transient model and validity check | Derived, screening-level; not a validated hardware prediction |

## Suggested pacing

Plan 60–100 minutes per core module and about 90 minutes for Module 7. A learner who wants a compact overview can complete Modules 1–3 and read the summaries of Modules 4–6; a learner developing a concept should complete the six core modules and use Module 7 when the heat load or sink changes in time.

For an independent, output-oriented route through the same material, follow the [self-paced study manual](self-paced-study-manual.md). It sequences the modules, specifies the study record and completion evidence, and preserves the distinction between a screening concept and a validated design.

## Optional capstone

Choose a real but non-confidential system: a small electronics enclosure, a laboratory instrument, a refrigerated cabinet, a battery pack, or a room-level heat source. Prepare the Module 6 concept memo. Clearly separate measured inputs from assumptions, and do not present the result as a validated design.
