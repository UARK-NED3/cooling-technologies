# Learning checks

Use these prompts after attempting each module. The answers show one defensible line of reasoning; equivalent answers are acceptable when their assumptions and units are explicit.

## Module 1

A 2 kW, 10-minute battery heat pulse is not enough to select a cooling method. Relevant missing inputs include: the allowable cell and module temperatures and spatial nonuniformity; the initial temperature and post-pulse recovery time; the ambient or available sink temperature; the repeated duty cycle; available mass, volume, power, water, and maintenance; and the actual location at which 2 kW is generated. A thermal mass may be plausible for an isolated pulse if it can absorb the energy and subsequently reject it; forced air or liquid cooling must also meet the peak heat-transfer and recovery requirements.

## Module 2

Halving the spreader resistance changes the series total from `0.27` to `0.25 K/W`, so the temperature rise becomes `30 K`. Halving the air-side resistance changes the total to `0.18 K/W`, so the rise becomes `21.6 K`. Reducing the larger air-side resistance therefore has the greater effect in this stated model. The conclusion can change if the components are not truly in series or the coefficients vary substantially with temperature or flow.

## Module 3

Examples of conditions that can rule out a mechanism:

- Passive air cooling: the required controlled temperature is below available air temperature at steady state, or the permitted thermal resistance cannot be achieved within the available area.
- Direct evaporative cooling: the required supply temperature is below the ambient wet-bulb limit, or water/hygiene constraints prohibit it.
- Liquid loop: a leak-intolerant environment, unavailable heat sink, unsuitable fluid/material compatibility, or unacceptable pump-power and maintenance burden can rule it out.

The relevant conditions are application-specific; the point is to state them rather than label a method intrinsically good or bad.

## Module 4

The 0.25 kg/s energy-balance estimate can be insufficient if (1) individual parallel branches receive unequal flow, producing a local hot spot, or (2) pressure-drop limits, pump performance, and fluid viscosity prevent the estimated flow from being delivered. Additional causes include contact resistance, inadequate heat-transfer area, gas entrainment, bypass, and a sink temperature higher than assumed.

## Module 5

Before comparing two reported COP values, check at least: (1) the cooling-duty boundary and electrical-input boundary, including auxiliaries; (2) source and sink temperatures, humidity, flow, and part-load state; and (3) the measurement or rating method and time basis. Fouling state, controls, and defrost or standby energy can also be material.

## Module 6

An adequate screening memo identifies the protected location, heat load, temperature requirement, sink condition, time basis, model assumptions, candidate architectures, selected concept, dominant uncertainty, and next validation action. If the memo cannot name an input that could change the selection, it is likely hiding uncertainty rather than managing it.
